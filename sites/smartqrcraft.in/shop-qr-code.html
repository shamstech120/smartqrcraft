How to Get a QR Code for Your Shop

There are two ways to get a payment QR code for a shop in India: make one yourself from your UPI ID in a couple of minutes, or apply for a merchant QR from a UPI app or bank. This page lets you do the first, and explains when the second is better.

Make your shop QR code now (2 minutes)

  1. Open your UPI app and copy your UPI ID. It looks like shopname@okaxis or 98xxxxxx10@ybl.
  2. Paste it into the UPI ID field above and add your shop name as the payee name.
  3. Leave the amount blank so customers type what they owe.
  4. Turn on the frame and write "Scan & Pay" or your shop name, then download the PNG.
  5. Print it, then pay ₹1 to yourself from a different phone to check the name and account are right.

Your own UPI QR vs a merchant QR

Both work with every UPI app. The difference is in the extras.

Which one should your shop use?

If you are starting out or take a handful of payments a day, a UPI QR from your own UPI ID is enough. Many small shops begin this way.

If you take many payments, want a speaker that announces each payment, or need clean business reports, apply for a merchant QR in the business version of your UPI app or at your bank. Banks and apps may also ask you to move regular business collections to a business account, so check your bank's terms.

You can use both: the merchant standee at the counter and a designed code from this page on your menu board, invoices or WhatsApp catalogue.

Print Tips

Printing your shop QR code

Size

At least 5 cm (2 in) wide for a counter; 10 cm or more for a wall behind the counter.

Lamination

Laminate it or use a plastic stand so it survives water and dust.

Placement

At chest height, near the billing area, with good light and no glare.

Name on it

Print your shop name next to the code so customers know they are paying the right shop.

Spare copy

Keep a second print in case the first is damaged.

Check weekly

Look for stickers pasted over your code. Replace it if anything looks changed.

Keep your shop QR code safe

  • Customers pay you; you never scan to receive money. If anyone asks you to scan a code or enter your PIN to receive a payment, it is a scam.
  • Always confirm the credit in your own app or on your speaker before handing over goods. A screenshot is not proof.
  • Watch for code swaps. Fraudsters sometimes paste their own QR code over a shop's code.
  • Your UPI ID is safe to share. It only lets people pay you. Never share your UPI PIN or OTPs.
