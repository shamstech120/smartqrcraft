In a shop, a QR code is a bridge from the physical product to something your shelf label cannot hold: a video, a manual, a review page or a way to pay.

QR code or barcode on products?

They do different jobs. A barcode (EAN or UPC) identifies a product at the checkout and in inventory systems. A QR code is for customers, giving them information on their phone. Most products can carry both. If you need internal barcodes for stock, see the barcode generator. Retail barcodes for selling through retailers need officially issued numbers.

Tips for retail QR codes

  • Give a reason to scan, like "Scan for the setup video".
  • Aim at mobile-friendly pages that load fast on store WiFi or mobile data.
  • Keep codes at least 2 to 3 cm wide on labels, and check the size guide.
  • Use stable links. Codes on packaging last as long as the product is on the shelf.
  • Test on real packaging, including glossy or curved surfaces.
  • Protect customers. Check counters and windows for stickers placed over your codes.
