Static vs Dynamic QR Codes: What Is the Difference?

The difference is where the destination lives. A static code contains it. A dynamic code points to a service that decides where to send people, which is why it can be edited and tracked.

The quick answer

A static QR code stores your content, for example a web address, directly in the pattern. Once it is made, it cannot be changed, it needs no server and it works as long as the destination does.

A dynamic QR code stores a short link to a redirect service. When someone scans it, the service forwards them to your real destination. You can change that destination later and often see how many people scanned.

When a static QR code is the right choice

Static codes suit anything that does not need to change or be counted.

  • WiFi details, contact cards, plain text and calendar events, which are stored in the code itself.
  • Links to pages you control, such as your website, menu, portfolio or social profile.
  • Business cards, stickers and packaging where you do not want to depend on a subscription.
  • Situations where you want a code to work for years without paying anyone.

When a dynamic QR code makes sense

Dynamic codes earn their cost when you need flexibility or measurement.

  • Large print runs where the destination might change, such as posters, packaging or campaigns.
  • Marketing where you want to know how many people scanned, and when.
  • Testing different landing pages.
  • Cases where a printed mistake would be expensive to fix.

Risks of dynamic codes to know about

The flexibility comes with trade-offs.

  • Dependence. If you stop paying, or the company closes, the codes you printed may stop working or show an ad.
  • Free plans can be short. Some services allow only a few dynamic codes, or free codes that expire.
  • An extra hop. The scan goes through a redirect, which adds a delay and a point of failure.
  • Privacy. The service records scans and can see device information.
  • Lock-in. Codes are tied to the provider's short links, so you cannot easily move them to another service.

A simple alternative: make your own redirect

You can get most of the flexibility of a dynamic code without a subscription. Choose a short address on a domain you own, for example yourbrand.com/menu. Point that address at your real page, and make a static QR code of the short address. If the destination changes later, you update where yourbrand.com/menu points, and the printed code keeps working.

This depends only on your own domain, not on a QR code company. It does not give scan counts, but your website analytics can count visits to the short address.

How to choose

Ask three questions:

  1. Might the destination change after printing? If yes, use a dynamic code or your own redirect.
  2. Do I need scan counts? If yes, dynamic. If not, static is enough.
  3. Am I comfortable depending on a company for years? If not, choose static or your own redirect.
