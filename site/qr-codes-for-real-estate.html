Putting a QR code on a yard sign

A sign is read by people walking or pulling over, not by drivers, so design for a pedestrian at the curb.

  • Size: for scanning from 1 to 2 metres, aim for about 10 to 20 cm. See the size guide.
  • Contrast: dark code on a white panel, even if the rest of the sign is colorful.
  • Call to action: "Scan for photos and price" beats a bare code.
  • Weather: use vinyl or a laminated panel, and choose a matte finish to avoid glare.
  • Reuse: a code that points to a page you control can be reused for the next listing if you update the page.

Where the code should send people

A phone user wants price, photos and a way to contact you quickly. Send them to a mobile-friendly listing page, not a desktop brochure or a large PDF. Put your phone number and a contact button near the top.

Because static QR codes cannot be edited, aim each code at a stable page. If the listing changes, update the page. For a property that sells, you can redirect that page to your next listing.

Check local rules

Advertising rules, sign regulations and your brokerage or listing board's requirements differ by place. Check them before you print signs and marketing. This page is general information, not legal advice.
